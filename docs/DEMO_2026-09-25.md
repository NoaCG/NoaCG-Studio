# The 2026-09-25 session - the script, and the gap list it is

**What this is.** The ordered beats of the 25 September session: students and YLE people trying
NoaCG, hands on. For every beat: who drives, the exact route in the product, and the evidence that
it works today or the word GAP with the next step named. The gap list in §7 is what the two weeks
before the date are planned from. It is written to be read cold by the owner on a phone, and by a
session on the morning of the 25th.

**The owner's brief** (2026-09-09, paraphrased from speech, so the intent binds and the wording
does not): the 12th is no longer the pressure. The date is the 25th. We owe one presentation and
one step-by-step guide over two capabilities - SVG import ending in their own graphics playing on
their systems, and the CLI taking a graphic from Codex or Claude Code to the NoaCG player in
minutes.

**Status legend, used in every table below.**

| WORKS, UNSEEN | on `main` and pinned; no person has ticked it on the surface the beat uses, or the last tick is older than the code |
| GAP | not there, or nothing evidences it; the next step is named in the row |

## 0. The shape, and the five calls it rests on

The brief says "trying NoaCG", so this is a follow-along session and not a demonstration. That
one word decides most of the shape.

1. **They drive.** Every beat a participant can do on their own laptop is theirs. We drive only
   the beats that need a playout box or a coding-agent subscription some of them will not have.
2. **Both roads end at the same place, so it is taught once.** An imported SVG and an agent-made
   graphic both become a library record, both go into a production, and both air through one
   output URL. Road 1 and road 2 stop at the library; §5 takes the production to air once, for
   both.
3. **Road 1 goes first.** It needs no terminal, no subscription and no account until the
   production is published, so the whole room can do it. Road 2 needs Node and, for its full
   form, an agent subscription.
4. **"Their systems" is four targets, prepared in advance, chosen on the day.** A browser source
   loading the output URL is the one every student can do at their desk and it is the one the
   evidence is strongest on. CasparCG 2.3 with the URL, CasparCG with a file, and an OGraf
   renderer are bring-your-box beats; each carries its own risk in §5.
5. **The step-by-step guide is `/docs`, not a second document.** A guide that lives beside the
   product drifts (the two live-vote guides already disagree, `docs/backlog/the-two-live-vote-guides-disagree.md`).
   What is printed is a one-page index into the pages, in beat order.

**"Row A" and "row D" below** are the two sibling rows of the 2026-09-09 day wave that were running
while this was written: A times the CLI path from scaffold to on air and fixes what slows it; D
walks the SVG import road as a stranger and writes the end-to-end `/docs` page. The wave plan is
not in the tree, so read their results in `docs/handoffs/2026-09-09-a-*.md` and
`docs/handoffs/2026-09-09-d-*.md` once they land.

The 2026-09-12 student production (quiz and scoreboard, drawn by students) still happens, and it
is the dress rehearsal for road 1 and for §5's hardware beats. Several UNSEEN rows below turn into
WORKS on the 12th for free.

**Timing.** About ninety minutes: 10 opening, 30 road 1, 25 road 2, 20 on air, 5 close. A beat
that runs long is cut at the next beat's boundary, never stretched.
