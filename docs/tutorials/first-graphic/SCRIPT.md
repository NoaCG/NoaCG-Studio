# Script: your first graphic

**Everything under a beat heading is spoken, word for word. Nothing else in this file is.**

Twelve beats, about 700 words, roughly five minutes at a calm pace. One voice throughout. The
frame named under each heading is what is on screen while those words are spoken;
`INSTRUCTIONS.md` says what happens inside it, and every line here was checked against the frame
beside it.

Two names to get right if the voice is synthetic. HJK is three letters, "hoo-yoo-koo" in Finnish
or "aitch jay kay" in English. Ilves is two syllables, "ILL-vess".

---

## Beat 1 - three doors
`step-1-import-door.png`

This is NoaCG Studio. You have a graphic you drew yourself, and in about five minutes it will be
on air with somebody typing into it.

Open the studio and it asks how you want to start. The door we want is the third one, Import
graphic. Your own artwork, no AI.

## Beat 2 - the canvas, then the file
`step-2-drop-zone.png`

Two things on this step, and the order matters.

First, the canvas. Sixteen by nine, nineteen twenty by ten eighty, twenty five frames a second.
If you drew something vertical, change it now, because once artwork is in, this row locks.

Second, before you drop anything, read the line above the drop zone: need help exporting SVG.
Open it once. These three rules decide whether the import works at all. Name your layers, because
those names become the labels your operator reads. Keep your text as text, and do not convert it
to outlines. One artboard, at the size you want on air.

Get those right in your design app and everything after this is easy.

## Beat 3 - what it found
`step-3-what-it-found.png`

Drop the file in. This one is a football scoreboard from the NoaCG practice library, and you can
download the same file and follow along.

NoaCG reads it straight back to you. The artwork is nineteen twenty by ten eighty. It found seven
text layers. It names the three typefaces the file asks for. Check that layer count against what
you drew: if it is lower than you expected, some of your type was turned into outlines on the way
out of your design app, and it is worth exporting again.

Notice the canvas row has changed its mind. It now says remove the current artwork before changing
its authored canvas. That is the lock.

And the walk just got shorter. Five steps now, not six. Your text is already exactly where you put
it, so there is nothing to erase and nothing to place.

## Beat 4 - fields
`step-4-fields.png`, then `step-4b-what-it-does.png` and `step-4c-pictures-and-fonts.png`

This is the step that turns a drawing into something an operator can drive.

Every text layer you drew is a row, grouped by the panel it sits on, and every row is already on.
The layer name is what your operator will read, so this is the moment to fix anything you called
Rectangle 3. The words beside it are what the field starts as, and the little grid says which edge
holds still when somebody types something longer.

Now look at what NoaCG worked out on its own. The two scores are plain figures, so they came in as
numbers. The match clock offers to be a countdown instead of text. Further down it says: two
numbers, each with a plus and a minus. Your scoreboard already works, and you have not chosen
anything.

Two more things down there. The crest is a picture, and it is the one row that arrives switched
off, so tick it if you want your operator swapping the badge. And all three typefaces are bundled
with NoaCG, which is what makes the graphic look the same on every machine it plays out from.

## Beat 5 - animation
`step-5-animation.png`

Animation is how the graphic comes on and how it goes off. Fade is already chosen. Click any of
the others and it plays in the preview beside you, so this is a step you can spend ten seconds on
or ten minutes.

## Beat 6 - finish
`step-6-finish.png`

Finish shows you everything you just built, and then asks for two names. They are not the same
name.

The first names the graphic. Call it Match scorebug. The second names a new production, which is
the show you are going to run it in. Call it Saturday Match. Name it for the show, not for this
graphic, because everything else you build for that show goes in beside it.

Then press add to the production.

## Beat 7 - the dashboard
`step-7-production.png`

This is the playout dashboard, and this is where the show is run.

Preview on the left is what you are checking. Program on the right is what your audience sees, and
it says nothing on air, because nothing is. Your graphic is already in the cue rundown, and its
fields are under the monitors, ready to type into.

## Beat 8 - take
`step-8-on-air.png`

Press Take. Space bar does it too.

That is your drawing, on air.

## Beat 9 - the scores act at once
`step-9-score-bumped.png`

Now the part a scoreboard exists for. Under the fields is a strip called live numbers, and it says
what it does: one press changes the figure on the live graphic, and no update is needed.

HJK score twice. And that away goal was offside, so take it back. Four nil, and every press was
already on air.

## Beat 10 - typing waits
`step-10-typed-not-on-air.png`

Typing is different, and this is the one thing to take away from the whole video.

Type a new team name into the cue and watch both monitors. Preview changes. Program does not.
Along the top of the fields, in amber: one change not on air yet, press Update.

Your audience is still looking at the old name, and it will keep looking at it for as long as you
leave it there.

## Beat 11 - update
`step-11-updated.png`

Press Update. Now program agrees with preview.

So: numbers act on their own press, typed values wait for Update. An operator who does not know
that airs the previous name and blames the graphic.

## Beat 12 - out
`step-12-off-air.png`

Out plays the graphic off. Nothing on air, and the cue is back to preview, ready to go again.

That is the whole road. Your own drawing, five steps, and four buttons. What you draw next is up
to you, and the practice library has a file for every kind of graphic, made to be taken apart.
