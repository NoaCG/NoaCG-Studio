# Fix 2667: the 60-graphic publish write sent every script twice

Issue #2667: on hosted staging a 60-graphic team publish (team_designs and production_copies on,
auto_publish and partial_writes off) took 64 s once and failed twice with "canceling statement due
to statement timeout" another day. Run issue #2361. Measured 2026-11-21 on the local stack.

## Answer in short

The press makes two kinds of large request: the team record's first split save (all 60 designs,
six `team_designs_put` calls of up to 1 MB) and the publish write to `control_shows`. The publish
write is the one statement near the 8 s limit. It sent 9.7 MB, and 4.6 MB of that was the `panel`
column repeating each graphic's script (about 76 KB per lower third) beside the payload's own copy.
PostgREST parses the whole request body inside the statement, so the statement grows with the bytes
sent: 3.0 to 3.4 s at staging-like slowness, before any other load. Nothing reads the panel's
script since the hosted control page was retired (docs/work-specs/retire-hosted-control). A publish
now leaves it out: 5.1 MB, 1.0 to 1.7 s.

## How it was measured

- Local stack: Supabase CLI 2.111.0 through `scripts/configured-stack.mjs`, all migrations, the
  configured suite's local account. Role limits as hosted: `authenticated` statement_timeout 8 s.
- Staging-like slowness: the database container limited to 0.1 CPU (`docker update --cpus 0.1`).
  At that limit a 5.1 MB team record insert takes 2.8 s in the database, about 9 times the 4-CPU
  time, a little slower than staging's 4.5 to 8 times (phase-9-load-diagnosis.md section 5).
- Requests: the step-7 bodies taken from the trace of staging run 44892116622 (`hosted-results-1`),
  replayed as the signed-in account; and the real page, a 60-graphic team production built as the
  phase 8 scene builds it, one Publish press, every backend request timed. Statement times from
  pg_stat_statements; the update broken down with EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS).
- Staging was not run: no staging switch was touched.

## Where the press's time goes (0.1 CPU, first publish, 60 graphics)

| From the press | team_designs on | team_designs off |
|---|---|---|
| team record save | 6 `team_designs_put` (0.4 to 1.2 s each, 5.1 MB in all), then `team_production_store` (26 KB, 0.1 s) | one `team_production_save_v2` (5.1 MB, 2.3 to 2.6 s) |
| browser work before the publish read | about 8.5 s (production gate and payload build for 60 graphics; no request) | about 8.5 s |
| publish write before | 9.7 MB, 3.6 s request, landed 18.9 s after the press | 9.7 MB, 3.2 s request, landed 17.4 s after the press |
| publish write after | 5.1 MB, 1.0 s request, landed 14.4 s after the press | 5.1 MB, 1.1 s request, landed 14.5 s after the press |

The save comes with the press because a first publish settles the production's outputs, which
changes the record.

## The statement

pg_stat_statements, 0.1 CPU, the trace's 60-graphic body:

| | before (9.7 MB) | after (5.1 MB) |
|---|---|---|
| INSERT (first publish) | 3.0 s | 1.0 s |
| UPDATE (republish, whole) | 2.9 to 3.4 s | 0.8 to 1.7 s |

The same UPDATE with the values already parsed in the database costs 0.1 to 0.4 s (EXPLAIN
ANALYZE: `control_publication_guard_b_output` 0.2 s, `control_shows_set_updated_at` 0.09 s, the
rest compression). Parsing the 9.7 MB body to jsonb alone takes 1.0 to 1.4 s. So most of the
statement is the body, and halving the body roughly halves it.

The design puts are not near the limit: 0.4 to 1.2 s each at 0.1 CPU for 1 MB. On staging the
trace showed 1.0 to 6.4 s per put, and 15.8 s for the 9.7 MB publish request.

## Why it failed on one day and not another

Staging was slower that day: the failing run's set-up took 634 s against 279 s, and its first
minute had a p95 of 10.5 s per request. With the publish statement at 3 s or more on an idle
staging-like database, a slower moment takes it past 8 s. After the fix the statement has about
five times headroom at this slowness instead of about two and a half.

## Not changed here

- The browser's 8.5 s before the publish read is client work, not a statement; on a slower runner
  it adds to the press time but cannot time out.
- With team_designs off, the press still saves the whole 5.1 MB record (2.3 to 2.6 s at 0.1 CPU);
  that is what team_designs exists to remove (#2614).
- Rows published before this change keep the script in `panel` until their next publish.
