# A room of 100 and 200 phones voting on staging, 2026-11-25

P1 AC-4 ("the cap is sized for the largest room a production expects, measured") and the AC-6 counts,
for a live production with about 100 phones the week after.

## How it ran

- **Staging only**, through `.github/workflows/hosted-latency.yml` with `spec=e2e/instruments/vote-load.ts`
  on branch `noacg/nice-wozniak-hp8gzj`, shard 1 (the other shards skip, so one room loads the
  database at a time). Production was not read or written.
- **Phones**: `scripts/vote-load.mjs`, one Node process on a GitHub runner. Each phone has its own
  device token and sends what `src/audience/audienceData.ts` sends: `audience_join_by_slug` every
  5 s with 10% jitter either way, `audience_vote` with the round and revision it last read, a re-read
  after every send, the publishable key only. A tenth of the room arrives during each run, a fifth
  changes its vote 2 to 6 s after voting, everyone else votes within 5 s of seeing the question. A
  phone that sees "Not sent" or "Too many votes at once" presses Vote again, twice at most.
- **Operator**: the real Interaction tab on a published staging production of a throwaway account,
  pressing Go live, Stop, Continue the count and Start a new count.
- **The six readings compared after each Stop**: the script's own count (each phone's last vote the
  server answered 200 to), the panel's counts, `audience_tally`, `audience_votes_export`, the CSV
  the panel downloads, and the result cue's values after Add results to rundown. Each phone's
  "Your vote" read-back is compared with what it sent.
- **Host**: the staging database's privileged metrics endpoint, every 5 s (CPU, disk, memory, load,
  connections). Staging is the free tier: 434 MiB of memory, shared CPU.

## Counts: every vote counted exactly once

Fifteen counts compared (the 100-phone room three times, the 200-phone room twice, three counts each):
all six readings agreed in every one, every phone read back the vote it sent, and every phone got its
vote in on the first press. 2,030 vote requests in all; none refused, none "Not sent".

| Room | Count | Votes | Six readings agree |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | First count | 100 | 3 of 3 runs |
| 100 | Continue the count | 110 | 3 of 3 |
| 100 | Start a new count | 120, the earlier count kept at 110 | 3 of 3 |
| 200 | First count | 200 | 2 of 2 |
| 200 | Continue the count | 220 | 2 of 2 |
| 200 | Start a new count | 240, the earlier count kept at 220 | 2 of 2 |

Continue the count added the late phones and kept the changed votes as changes; Start a new count
started from zero and every phone voted again. The export had one voter key per counted phone. After
Stop, every phone showed the waiting page within 4 to 5 s (one poll).

## Response times

Measured from the runner, per room over its three counts, in ms (median / p95 / slowest):

| Run | Room | Votes | Join reads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 100 | 147 / 191 / 430 | 146 / 218 / 1041 |
| 4 | 200 | 147 / 220 / 1176 | 144 / 446 / 1422 |
| 3 | 100 | 191 / 499 / 1049 | 190 / 538 / 990 |
| 3 | 200 | 189 / 233 / 585 | 187 / 3829 / 12003 |
| 1 | 100 | 180 / 24726 / 28817 | 134 / 20258 / 28867 |

- **Run 4**: nothing slower than 1.5 s.
- **Run 3, 200 phones**: one stretch of about ten seconds after the third count, when the instrument's
  own check makes every phone read once more inside two seconds (about 140 requests a second with the
  polls). Join reads queued for up to 12 s, and so did the operator page's reads (`audience_tally` up
  to 9.9 s, `audience_list` up to 10 s); votes in that count stayed under 0.6 s. The same burst in run
  4 stayed under 1.5 s.
- **Run 1, 100 phones**: slow throughout with the same counts; in Continue the count the median vote
  took 24 s. One join read failed with "canceling statement due to statement timeout" (HTTP 500; a
  vote failing that way shows "Not sent" and the person presses again). The host's load average
  reached 3.95, against under 0.9 in runs 3 and 4.

## The staging database while it ran

Over each room (runs 3 and 4): CPU about 9 to 11% busy, disks 12 to 17% busy, memory at most 341 of
434 MiB, load average at most 0.89, at most 21 connections. The slow stretches did not show as a busy
host, so requests waited in front of the database (the free tier's API gateway or its REST server's
connection pool), not in it. The endpoint refreshes its counters about once a minute, so a
20-second count has no CPU figure of its own.

## What it means for about 100 phones

- Counting is right. No vote was lost, counted twice, refused or left unsent, at 100 or 200 phones.
- The burst cap (3000 phone votes per question in 10 s) is far above this room: 240 votes in a count
  at most, none refused.
- The risk is time, not loss. On a slow backend a vote can take seconds, once almost half a minute,
  to come back, and the phone keeps its Vote button grey meanwhile with nothing said. The vote is
  counted when it lands. Staging is the free tier; production runs on a larger compute size, and was
  not measured here.
