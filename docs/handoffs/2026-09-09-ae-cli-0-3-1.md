## What actually changed, and why each one mattered

**`noacg types` fits a terminal.** It was 67 rows whose widest line was 354 characters, so every
long row wrapped two or three times and the columns stopped lining up - which destroys the only
information a table carries. `typesTable()` now keeps `type` and `neutral` whole, because those
are the columns a reader chooses by, and splits what is left across fields, events and designs in
proportion to their natural widths, cutting at a whole item with the count it dropped (`+7`).
Measured against the live deployment's 67 types, the widest line is exactly the terminal width at
40, 60, 80, 100, 120, 160, 200 and 400 columns, with one column layout across every row.

**The design decision inside it, in case anyone wants to revisit it.** I split the three list
columns proportionally rather than declaring a favourite. The alternative the backlog file
suggested was to keep `events` at its natural width and squeeze `fields` and `designs`, and I
rejected it: `events` is 57 characters at its widest, which is more than half the whole budget at
100 columns, and keeping it whole would be a claim that events matter more than fields when you
are choosing a type. I do not think they do. The owner-queue item asks him to look at the result
and say if he disagrees.

**The stray-word refusal reaches `login` and `caspar`.** `a997eabe` closed this on the verbs that
take a package; two worse victims were still open. `login --name My Laptop` named the machine's
key "My" - reproduced on 0.3.0 before touching anything, the consent URL carried `name=My` and the
word "Laptop" was never mentioned again. That name is the only thing telling one machine's key
from another's in Settings when the user comes to revoke one. And `caspar play --url … 1 20` reads
as "channel 1, layer 20" and is not: the two words were dropped and the PLAY went out on the
default channel and layer, which is the one command family here that reaches live playout
hardware. `doctor`, `docs`, `logout`, `whoami`, `types` and `mcp` had the same hole and are closed
in the same pass, so the grammar `docs/AGENT_CLI.md` describes is now actually complete.

## Evidence and traps that exist in no repo file

- **The delegate could not run anything, and its self-report said so wrongly.** Codex delivered
  the types table and its tests cleanly (`gpt-5.6-sol`, high, recorded in the outcome ledger as
  `clean`), but reported "61 passed, 1 failed" from a sandbox `EPERM` on the credential-store
  test, and could not reach the browser queue at all. Both were sandbox artefacts: the same
  commands here are 70/0 and the live commands run fine. **Read a delegate's numbers as a claim
  about its sandbox, not about the code** - I re-derived every one of them, including rendering
  the real 67-type payload at eight widths, and the code was right where the report was wrong.
- **`process.stdout.columns` is `undefined` under a pipe, and that nearly shipped a regression.**
  My own spec to the delegate said "fall back to 100 when piped". The review caught what that
  meant: `cli/skill/noacg-graphic/SKILL.md` sends an agent to a bare `noacg types` for the design
  id it then scaffolds with, an agent's stdout is always a pipe, and the skill mentions `--json`
  nowhere. So the fitted table would have shown an agent two of eight design ids. A width is now
  a real terminal width or nothing, and nothing means the full table. If anyone later "tidies"
  that fallback back in, they re-break the agent path silently.
- **The stray-word refusal used to advise quoting unconditionally, and that advice is often
  wrong.** `caspar stop nonsense` told the operator to add quotes when the fix is to delete the
  word, and the example offered for `caspar status` proposed a hostname with spaces in it. The
  advice is now conditional on there being real quoting to show.
- **Nothing here needed the dev server.** Every walk ran the CLI against `https://noacg.studio`,
  which is what a stranger does, and `noacg doctor` connects straight to the live bridge with the
  key already stored on this machine. Worth remembering next time a CLI row assumes it needs
  `npm run dev:worktree`.

## The finding I did not fix, and what the next session should NOT do

The walk's third finding - three of six neutral scaffolds warn on their own stress bench - is
still open, deliberately. It is a studio template fault rather than a CLI one, its files are
outside this row's TOUCHES, and it was the row's declared cut-first item. But I reproduced it and
the reproduction changes what the work is, so
`docs/backlog/neutral-scaffolds-fail-their-own-stress-bench.md` is rewritten around measurements
instead of guesses (`10b6f05b`).

**The short version, because the item used to say the opposite: do not touch the template CSS.**
An untouched neutral scoreboard still warns, but its `stress.png` shows the plate grown around the
doubled text with `#f1` 24px clear of the box's right edge and 83px clear of its bottom. There is
no overflow in the picture at all. Driving the exported package's own runtime through the bench's
exact stress sequence - `play()`, `update()` with the doubled values, sample at the 120 ms and
80 ms the bench waits - `#f1` never leaves `.scoreboard-box`; the worst of four samples is 23.2px
INSIDE, against a +2px threshold. The `update()` mask pop was the last plausible way a rectangle
could escape and it is at scale 0.966 by the time the measurement is taken.

So the fault is in the bench, not the scaffold. The item now points at check (a2) in
`overflowIssues`, `src/validation/runtimeBench.ts`, and notes that its neighbour check (a)
deliberately exempts `-mask` ancestors while (a2) does not - though that is a reading of the code,
not a measurement, and the file says so. **The next session's first move is to instrument the
bench where it measures and print the two rectangles it is comparing**, through the studio's own
bridge, which is the one thing my reproduction could not use. If the cause turns out to be the
bench, the catalog designs are affected too and nobody has looked.
