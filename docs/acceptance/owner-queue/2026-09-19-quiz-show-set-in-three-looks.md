# A quiz show set in three looks: quiz board, two-player score, lower third

## What changed

Three new style families are in the catalog, each with the three graphics a two-player quiz
show needs. Sticker is neo-brutal paper labels with thick outlines and hard shadows. Showtime is
a theatre marquee with bulbs, deep pills and a serif. Arcade is a pixel-cornered neon cabinet
screen with scanlines.

- **Quiz show board** (Sticker Quiz, Showtime Quiz, Arcade Quiz). Three moments: the question,
  the contestant's pick, the correct answer. There is no lock step. "Answers shown" is a field
  with 2, 3 or 4, so one graphic serves every question and the board keeps its width. The
  control page has Pick A, Pick B, Pick C, Pick D, Clear pick and Reveal correct answer.
- **Two-player score** (Sticker Score, Showtime Score, Arcade Score). Point to player 1 or 2,
  take one back, Final score marks the leader, New game puts both to 0. Top of the frame by
  default, and the Position grid in the Style step moves it to the bottom.
- **Lower third** (Sticker Strap, Showtime Strap, Arcade Strap), two lines and an optional logo.

## Route in under a minute

Open `/app` -> Start from a template -> press the **Sticker**, **Showtime** or **Arcade** style
chip. Three cards appear. Pick the quiz -> Next. On the Fields step the Setup section offers
"Correct answer" and "Answers shown". Finish into the editor, open the Control tab, press Take,
then Pick C, Pick A, Reveal correct answer.

For the score: same chip -> the Score card -> in the Style step press the bottom-centre cell of
the Position grid to see it at the bottom. In the Control tab press Point to player 1 a few
times, then Final score, then New game.

## What to look at

- Are the three looks different enough that choosing between them feels like a real decision?
- On each quiz board, can you tell the pick, the correct answer and a wrong pick apart without
  relying on colour? Each one also changes shape: a pressed-in label, a second ring, a blinking
  cursor, a drawn tick, a hatch, a dashed edge.
- Set "Answers shown" to 2 and to 3. The Sticker board puts the third answer across the full
  width. Does each count look designed, not like a board with a hole in it?
- Does the score strip read at the top and at the bottom next to a lower third?
- Showtime's bulbs chase in steps and Arcade's pick cursor blinks. Too much for a whole show?
